# Tenbagger — Design Psychology & Visual Direction

*Researched 2026-09-25 via web search (snippets; full pages not fetched). Evidence tags: **[P]** primary source (company newsroom, regulator, peer-reviewed paper), **[S]** secondary (press, analyst, trade blog), **[U]** unverified / vendor or aggregator claim — treat as directional only.*

**The one-sentence thesis:** Tenbagger should feel like a *collectible field notebook for a young analyst* — warm, tactile and characterful when you learn, calm and matter-of-fact when you look at your own money — and it should never reward trading.

## 2. Psychology principles → UI applications

| # | Principle | Evidence | Concrete Tenbagger UI |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Self-determination theory** (competence, autonomy, relatedness) | Meta-analysis: gamification increases intrinsic motivation, autonomy and relatedness, with *minimal* effect on competence [P] link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11423-023-10337-7 (2023). The overall gamified-learning effect is small but significant [P] link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w (2019) | **Autonomy:** choose your "beat" (the sectors or companies you want to learn through, e.g. Nike, Spotify, Chipotle). **Competence:** "Explain it back" checks on real 10-K numbers, not just XP. **Relatedness:** campus clubs and study-buddy streaks. Don't rely on points alone to build competence. |
| 2 | **Goal-gradient** | Café card: purchases accelerate as the reward nears [P] Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng 2006, JMR, journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1509/jmkr.43.1.39 | The lesson progress bar *speeds up visually* near the end. Unit maps show "2 lessons to your next card". The money hub shows "$38 to cover your card by the 14th" as a filling bar. |
| 3 | **Endowed progress** | Car wash: 34% vs 19% completion with 2 pre-stamped slots, and only when a *reason* is given [S] researchgate.net (Nunes & Drèze 2006) | Onboarding: "You already know 2 of 10 concepts: you've used Spotify and bought Chipotle" pre-fills the first unit. Always state the reason. |
| 4 | **Zeigarnik / collection completion** | Incomplete tasks stay salient; complete-the-set motivates (Pokémon boom [S] ypulse.com 2025-08-26) | "Company Cards" binder with empty silhouettes; set bonuses ("the full Streaming set: NFLX, SPOT, ROKU"). Resume with "1 question left" on the home screen. |
| 5 | **Peak-end rule** | Kahneman et al.; experiences are judged by their peak and their end [S, classic] | The lesson-complete moment is the product's signature: the card flips with haptics, foil shimmer and a *one-line insight about a real company* ("You just read Chipotle's margins better than most adults"). End every session on a win, never on a quiz miss. |
| 6 | **IKEA effect** | Self-builders bid ~63% more; the effect vanishes if the task fails or is destroyed [P] Norton, Mochon & Ariely 2012, JCP; hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/11-091.pdf | Users *build* their own "Personal 10-K" and their own screener "thesis cards". Make building completable in under 2 minutes, because failed builds kill the effect. |
| 7 | **Identity-based motivation** | People act in line with the identity they hold (Oyserman) [S, classic]. Wrapped's archetypes drive sharing [S] musicbusinessworldwide.com 2025-12 | Rank titles: *Intern → Analyst → Senior Analyst → PM*. The copy says "analysts check the cash flow statement first". The profile is an "Analyst ID badge". |
| 8 | **Social proof & shareable artifacts (Wrapped effect)** | Wrapped 2025: 500M shares on day one [S] | Share cards: "My Analyst Type: The Moat Hunter", semester recaps, a completed card set. **Never share P&L or balances**, and every share template is privacy-safe by default. |
| 9 | **Variable rewards (ethical)** | Unpredictable rewards increase engagement but underpin addictive design; regulators flagged scratch-off rewards (§3) | Vary only *content surprises* (a fun-fact card, a rare "foil" when you answer a hard question correctly), and **only for learning actions**. No money, no stock, no randomised prizes tied to deposits or trades. |
| 10 | **Loss aversion (streak freeze)** | Streak Freeze reportedly reduced at-risk churn 21% [U] strivecloud.io | Give streak freezes generously (earned by learning). Add "Weekend Mode" and a finals-week pause. Copy says "Your streak is safe" and never "Duo is sad". |
| 11 | **Fresh start effect** | Gym visits rise after temporal landmarks: +33% at the start of a week, +47% at the start of a semester [P] Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014, Management Science, pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901 | "New Semester, New Ledger": prompts at the semester start, the 1st of the month and after a paycheck. A missed streak becomes "Chapter 2 starts today". |
| 12 | **Money anxiety / shame-free design** | 59% of Gen Z are stressed or anxious about money; ~40% feel it all or most of the time [S] finance.yahoo.com ("5 reasons Gen Z feels most financial stress", 2025) / Credit One study; 72% of young adults are taking action on higher costs [P] newsroom.bankofamerica.com (2025-07); Gen Z treats finance as self-care [S] forbes.com (2025-02-18) | See §2.1. |

### 2.1 Designing for money anxiety (the money hub)

- **Lead with the answer, then the plan.** "Card due Oct 14: **you're covered** ($412 available vs $356 due)" or "**$38 short**, and here are 3 ways to close it." Never show a bare deficit number.
- **No red-number panic.** Show shortfalls in a warm amber or neutral ink with an action button, never red plus an exclamation mark. Reserve red for true errors (a failed connection) only.
- **Calm framing for bad news:** plain words, a time horizon ("over 12 months the market was up; this week it's down 3%"), and a *next step*. Delay the number by a beat and put a sentence above it.
- **Frame the balance sheet as a 10-K** to add professional distance: "Liabilities: $1,240 (student card)" reads as analysis, not judgement. The same rigour used on Apple is applied to *you*, and that feels empowering rather than shaming.
- **Companion tone = Finch, not Duolingo.** The mascot never guilt-trips about money. It's allowed to be cheeky about *lessons*.
- **Opt-in visibility:** hide totals on launch (tap to reveal), with a "private mode" blur. That's important in dorms and shared screens.
- **Celebrate behaviours, not balances:** "You checked your due date 3 days early" gets a sticker. A bigger balance gets nothing extra, because a balance reflects income, not virtue.

## 3. Ethics & regulation guardrails

**Case law and guidance**
- **Massachusetts v. Robinhood** (consent order Jan 18 2024): a $7.5M penalty. The complaint cited **confetti on trades, "popular stocks" lists, push notifications, and free-stock rewards revealed by a lottery-style "scratch-off"**. Robinhood agreed to permanently cease confetti [S] insurancejournal.com/news/east/2024/01/19/756246.htm; bostonglobe.com (2024-01-18); velaw.com.
- **FTC "Bringing Dark Patterns to Light"** (staff report, Sept 14 2022) names four common tactics: misleading or disguised ads; hard-to-cancel subscriptions; buried terms and junk fees; tricking consumers into sharing data [P] ftc.gov/reports/bringing-dark-patterns-light.
- **SEC predictive-data-analytics / DEP rule** (proposed 2023) was **withdrawn June 12 2025** [P] federalregister.gov/documents/2025/06/17/2025-11110. Federal pressure is lower, but **state regulators (MA) and the FTC still act**, and FINRA keeps publishing on gamification and social-media investing [P] finra.org (2022 gamification deck; 2025-12 social-media report); IOSCO's DEP report FR/07/2025 [P] iosco.org/library/pubdocs/pdf/IOSCOPD794.pdf.

**Hard "never" list for Tenbagger**
1. No celebration, XP, streaks, badges or sounds attached to **trades, deposits or account funding**. Celebrate *learning* only.
2. No loot boxes, scratch-offs, spin-wheels, mystery packs, or any randomised reward with monetary value.
3. No purchasable card packs and no tradable or resellable cards (the NBA Top Shot failure mode). Cards are earned, never bought.
4. No "trending / most-bought by users" lists that nudge herd buying. Use "most-*studied* this week" instead.
5. No leaderboards on returns or portfolio value. Leaderboards cover learning XP within a club only, and are opt-in.
6. No push notifications triggered by price moves that imply urgency ("NVDA is tanking, act now").
7. No fake urgency or countdowns on subscriptions; one-tap cancel (FTC "click-to-cancel" spirit); price shown before the trial.
8. No pre-checked data-sharing or bank-linking consent. Plaid linking is explicit and explainable.
9. No confirmshaming ("No thanks, I like being broke").
10. No simulated-trading P&L presented as real-looking gains without clear "paper" labelling, and no "you would have made $X" hindsight nudges.
11. No guilt-based streak copy about money. Streak freezes are free and plentiful.
12. Screener and lesson content is **educational, not a recommendation**: no "buy" colouring and no star ratings that look like analyst ratings.

## 4. Colour & type psychology

**Trust vs excitement.** Blues and deep greens signal stability and trust, which is why banks use them. Saturated yellow-green, coral and violet signal energy and youth (Robinhood neon, Monzo coral, Partiful). **Split by mode:** the learning side is allowed high-energy accents; the money hub uses one trusted base plus neutrals. Robinhood's 2024 rebrand shows that *restraint plus one signature accent* is how a youth brand earns adult trust [P].

**Gain/loss encoding (colourblind-safe).** About 8% of men and 0.5% of women have colour-vision deficiency, mostly red-green [S] visioncenter.org; pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10475627. Rules:
- Never rely on hue alone: always pair colour with **▲/▼ glyphs, +/− signs, and position**.
- Default palette **blue (up) / orange (down)** with an optional "classic green/red" setting. Check with a CVD simulator and a greyscale test; keep contrast ≥4.5:1 for text and ≥3:1 for chart marks (WCAG).
- In the money hub, "down" is **neutral ink + ▼**, not red. Red is reserved for errors.

**Dark mode.** 73% of Gen Z prefer dark mode (Adobe, via [S] newsfilecorp.com/release/252131, 2025); ~76% of US 18–24 smartphone users [U] forms.app. **Ship dark-first with a first-class light mode.** Use warm off-black (#121110-ish) rather than pure black, and desaturate accents ~10–15% on dark.

**Type.**
- One **characterful display face** for headings, card titles and recaps. A soft grotesk or a quirky serif conveys personality (e.g. a variable face that can "bounce" on the lesson-complete moment).
- A **tabular-figure** sans for all numbers (Inter / IBM Plex Sans / Geist with `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`). Numbers must align in balance sheets.
- An optional **mono** for "ticker" micro-labels, which adds analyst credibility.
- Kinetic type only at peaks (lesson end, recaps). Static elsewhere, and respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

### Recommended hybrid: **"Field Notebook + Company Cards", with a small companion**

- **Frame:** the whole app is your *Analyst's Notebook*. Home is a notebook desk (a bento-ish spread of "pages": today's lesson, your card binder, your money ledger).
- **Learning mode (energetic):** lessons are annotated 10-K pages. Finishing a company unit **mints a Company Card** (a card flip with foil, haptics and one real insight), which is the peak-end moment. Set completion drives Zeigarnik pull. Earned stickers decorate your notebook (IKEA effect).
- **Companion:** a small hand-drawn creature, "the Bagger", is your study buddy. It is cheeky in lessons, gentle and supportive in the money hub (Finch-style), never a guilt-trip.
- **Money hub mode (calm):** same notebook, but a clean **ledger page**: "Personal 10-K" with Assets / Liabilities / Cash-flow statement; the answer first ("You're covered"); amber, never red; tap-to-reveal totals.
- **Palette:** warm ink-black / paper-cream base; one signature accent (e.g. an electric "highlighter" chartreuse-yellow, the analyst's highlighter pen) plus a secondary coral for stickers; blue/orange + glyphs for up/down.
- **Type:** a characterful serif or grotesk display for headings, a tabular sans for numbers, mono micro-labels.
- **Share artifacts:** "Analyst ID" badge, completed card sets, "Semester in Review" (Wrapped-style kinetic story with an analyst archetype). Never balances or P&L.
- **Why this wins:** it's ownable (nobody in fintech looks like a notebook plus cards), it matches the product's truth (real SEC filings = real analyst work), it satisfies SDT (autonomy in choosing companies, competence via real numbers, relatedness via clubs and sharing), and it keeps excitement on *learning* while the money side stays calm and compliant.
