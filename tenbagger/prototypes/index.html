Three directions for Tenbagger

Each prototype has a clickable phone app (Learn path, a playable 3-question lesson, a screener with presets, a company page and the Money hub) next to a landing hero, and works in light and dark. Lesson, screener and company numbers are real, from data/companies.json and data/lessons.json. Money hub numbers belong to a fictional persona, Alex, 20.

Pros and cons

DirectionBest forProsCons
A · LedgerCredibility with students, parents and teachers (the Edu tier); the “read a 10-K” promise.
  • Cheapest to ship: tokens.ts already uses this palette, so it is mostly type and layout.
  • Reads as trustworthy and not “trading app”, which lowers the gamification and advice risk.
  • Ruled statements suit the Money hub and the planned Personal 10-K report.
  • Calm means less daily pull, which tests red-team risk #1: “finance lessons are homework”.
  • Editorial finance brands look alike, so it is harder to stand out in screenshots.
  • Needs the streak and league mechanics added without breaking the tone.
B · ArcadeHabit and D7 retention (the day-77 gate), beginners, share cards and virality.
  • Streaks, leagues, quests and hearts are visible on every screen, the mechanics Duolingo's published results rest on.
  • Screener Quests make the screener a game rather than a tool.
  • Due-date check as a clear “covered / heads up” badge is easy to read at a glance.
  • Most regulatory exposure: XP must come only from learning (Mass. v. Robinhood line).
  • Can feel juvenile to interview-prep buyers and the $49 Recruiting Pass.
  • Most illustration and animation work for a solo founder.
C · Terminal LiteAmbitious students, finance-club and interview-prep buyers; the screener as the “arena”.
  • Screener and company pages feel like real tools (query view, sparklines, sortable tables, keyboard shortcuts).
  • Shows the raw line items behind each question, which builds skill and trust.
  • The runway chart makes the coverage check feel analytical, not like budgeting.
  • Intimidating for beginners and for the Money-hub student persona.
  • Dark-first is harder for App Store and marketing screenshots, and weaker for daily-habit warmth.
  • Heavy use of mono type makes long explanations slower to read.
Designer's note.

These are separate directions, but the parts combine. A common path is A's type and palette as the base, with B's streak, league and quest components in a quieter style, and C's dense table and chart treatment reserved for Pro screener and company views.
